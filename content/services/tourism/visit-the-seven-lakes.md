# Visit the Seven Lakes of San Pablo City

San Pablo City, known as the **City of Seven Lakes**, is home to seven natural freshwater crater lakes that are central to the city's identity, livelihood, and tourism. The lakes are recognized as significant ecological zones and are protected under local and national environmental laws.

## The Seven Lakes

| Lake                | Location / Barangay           | Notable Features                                                                 |
| ------------------- | ----------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Sampaloc Lake**   | Barangay II (City Center)     | Largest lake; commercial fishpens; accessible from the city                      |
| **Pandin Lake**     | Barangay Sta. Maria Magdalena | Pristine waters; twin lake with Yambo (not part of the seven); bamboo raft tours |
| **Bunot Lake**      | Barangay Concepcion           | Scenic; surrounded by coconut trees                                              |
| **Inya Lake**       | Barangay San Lorenzo          | Quiet and serene; agricultural surroundings                                      |
| **Palakpakin Lake** | Barangay San Lorenzo          | Largest among the outer lakes; community fishing                                 |
| **Calibato Lake**   | Barangay Sta. Catalina        | Community use; aquaculture                                                       |
| **Mabini Lake**     | Barangay Sto. Angel           | Smallest of the seven lakes                                                      |

## Activities at the Lakes

- **Bamboo raft tours** — most popular at Pandin Lake; local boatmen offer guided lake crossings
- **Fishing and aquaculture** — regulated fishing and fishpen visits
- **Nature walks and trekking** — trails around Bunot, Pandin, and the outer lakes
- **Bird watching** — various migratory and resident bird species around the lake edges
- **Photography and sightseeing** — scenic views of all seven lakes

## How to Get There

**From Manila / SLEX:**
Take SLEX to the San Pablo City exit. From the San Pablo City exit, proceed to the City Hall area for Sampaloc Lake, or follow signages to reach the outer lakes.

**Public transportation from Manila:**
Ride a bus going to San Pablo City from either Buendia (Gil Puyat), Pasay, or Alabang terminals. Travel time is approximately 2–3 hours.

**From Laguna / Los Baños:**
Take a jeepney or bus heading to San Pablo City along the national highway. The ride from Los Baños takes approximately 45 minutes.

**Within San Pablo City:**
Tricycles and jeepneys serve routes to the different lakes. Habal-habal (motorcycle taxis) can reach some of the less accessible outer lakes.

## Entrance Fees and Guidelines

Entrance and activity fees vary per lake and are subject to change. Verify current fees with the City Tourism Office before visiting.

General guidelines for lake visitors:

- Do not throw garbage or dispose of waste into or near the lakes.
- Swimming is regulated and may be prohibited in certain areas due to safety and environmental concerns. Ask local guides before entering the water.
- Fishing within fishpens or commercial fishery areas is not permitted without proper authorization.
- Respect local communities and fishing families along the lakeshores.
- Bring your own water and pack out your trash.

## Best Time to Visit

San Pablo City can be visited year-round. The coolest and driest months (November to February) are ideal for outdoor lake activities and trekking. Avoid visiting during peak typhoon months (July to October) when trails and lake conditions may be unsafe.
