# Visit the Seven Lakes — San Pablo City

A comprehensive guide to visiting the **Seven Lakes** of San Pablo City — natural freshwater crater lakes central to the city's identity and tourism.

## What

San Pablo City, known as the **City of Seven Lakes**, is home to **seven natural freshwater crater lakes** that are central to the city's identity, livelihood, and tourism. The lakes are recognized as significant ecological zones and are protected under local and national environmental laws.

The Seven Lakes are: **Sampaloc, Palakpakin, Bunot, Pandin, Yambo, Muhikap (Mohicap), and Calibato**.

> Note: Local references sometimes list "Inya" or "Mabini" lakes — these are alternate names or related lakes in the same volcanic system.

## Who can visit

Open to:

- **Local residents** — free / minimal entry fees
- **Tourists from across the Philippines and abroad**
- **Families and groups** for day trips and overnight stays
- **Researchers and students** — ecology, geography, history
- **Photographers and content creators** (some areas require permits)
- **Religious pilgrims** (Mt. Banahaw connection)
- **School field trips** (coordinate with City Tourism Office)
- **Eco-tourists** for nature appreciation

## When to visit

| Season | Conditions |
|---|---|
| **November–February** (cool/dry) | **Best time** for trekking, raft tours, and outdoor activities |
| **March–May** (hot/dry) | Lake season — but very hot |
| **June–October** (rainy) | Avoid during typhoons; flash floods possible |
| **Weekends and holidays** | Crowded but lively |
| **Weekdays** | Peaceful, more peaceful raft tours |
| **Early morning** | Best photography light |
| **Late afternoon** | Sunset views over Sampaloc Lake |

## Where — The Seven Lakes

| Lake | Location / Barangay | Notable Features |
|---|---|---|
| **Sampaloc Lake** | Barangay II (City Center) | Largest lake; commercial fishpens; promenade walk |
| **Pandin Lake** | Barangay Sta. Catalina | Pristine waters; popular bamboo raft tours; twin with Yambo |
| **Yambo Lake** | Barangay Sta. Catalina | Twin lake with Pandin; less developed; pristine |
| **Bunot Lake** | Barangay Concepcion | Scenic; surrounded by coconut trees |
| **Palakpakin Lake** | Barangay San Lorenzo | Largest among outer lakes; community fishing |
| **Muhikap (Mohicap) Lake** | Barangay San Buenaventura | Smaller; serene; less visited |
| **Calibato Lake** | Barangay Sta. Catalina | Community use; aquaculture |

## Why visit

- **Unique geological feature** — Volcanic crater lakes are rare worldwide
- **Cultural identity** — Central to San Pablo's heritage as "City of Seven Lakes"
- **Eco-tourism** — Responsible tourism supports lake communities
- **Scenic beauty** — Crater lakes surrounded by coconut groves and mountains
- **Local cuisine** — Fresh tilapia, bangus from lake fishpens
- **Bamboo raft experience** — Iconic Pandin Lake tours
- **Bird watching** — Migratory and resident species
- **Trekking** — Trails around outer lakes
- **Pilgrimage proximity** — Near Mt. Banahaw

## Activities at the Lakes

### 🛶 Bamboo raft tours
- **Most popular at Pandin Lake**
- Guided lake crossings by local boatmen
- Includes traditional snacks (lakefish lunch package available)
- Life vests provided
- Fee paid directly to boatmen / community organization

### 🎣 Fishing and aquaculture
- Regulated fishing in designated areas
- Fishpen visits at Sampaloc and Palakpakin
- Buy fresh fish directly from fishermen
- **Note:** No fishing in protected zones without authorization

### 🌳 Nature walks and trekking
- Trails around Bunot, Pandin, Yambo
- Easy to moderate difficulty
- Local guides recommended
- Bring water and proper footwear

### 🐦 Bird watching
- Migratory and resident species
- Best at dawn and dusk
- Bring binoculars
- Bunot and Pandin lakes are especially good

### 📸 Photography and sightseeing
- Scenic views of all seven lakes
- **Sampaloc Lake Promenade** for accessible photography
- Drone use requires CAAP and city coordination

## How to get there

### From Manila / SLEX
1. Take SLEX to the **San Pablo City exit**
2. From the exit, proceed to **City Hall area** for Sampaloc Lake
3. Follow signages or hire local transport for outer lakes

### Public transportation from Manila
- Ride a bus to **San Pablo City** from:
  - **Buendia (Gil Puyat)** — JAC Liner, DLTB, others
  - **Pasay** — Various provincial buses
  - **Alabang** — Local terminals
- Travel time: **2–3 hours**

### From Laguna / Los Baños
- **Jeepney or bus** along the national highway to San Pablo City
- Travel time from Los Baños: **~45 minutes**

### Within San Pablo City
| To | Transport |
|---|---|
| **Sampaloc Lake** | Walking from City Hall, tricycle |
| **Pandin / Yambo lakes** | Tricycle to Sta. Catalina, then short hike |
| **Bunot Lake** | Tricycle/jeepney to Concepcion |
| **Outer lakes** | Tricycle, jeepney, or habal-habal |

## Entrance fees and guidelines

Entrance and activity fees vary per lake and may change. **Always verify current rates with the City Tourism Office before visiting.**

### General visitor guidelines

- **Don't litter** — pack out your trash
- **Swimming is regulated** — may be prohibited in certain areas; ask local guides
- **No fishing in fishpens or commercial areas** without authorization
- **Respect local communities** along the lakeshores
- **Bring your own water**
- **Use life vests** during raft tours
- **Drone usage** requires permits and coordination

## Best time to visit

San Pablo City can be visited year-round. The **coolest and driest months (November to February)** are ideal for outdoor lake activities and trekking.

**Avoid visiting during peak typhoon months (July to October)** when trails and lake conditions may be unsafe.

## What to bring

- **Water** (at least 1L per person)
- **Snacks / packed lunch**
- **Sun protection** (hat, sunscreen, sunglasses)
- **Insect repellent**
- **Comfortable walking shoes / sandals**
- **Swim attire** (if swimming is allowed)
- **Towel**
- **Cash** (small bills for fees and food)
- **Phone with offline maps** (signal may be weak at outer lakes)
- **Trash bag** for your waste

## Safety reminders

- **Don't swim alone** — no lifeguards at most lakes
- **Check weather** before heading out
- **Use life vests** for water activities
- **Inform someone** of your itinerary
- **Stay with local guides** in unfamiliar areas
- **Avoid lake edges** without barriers, especially with children
