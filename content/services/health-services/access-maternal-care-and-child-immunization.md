# Access Maternal Care and Child Immunization — San Pablo City

A guide to maternal, prenatal, postnatal, and child immunization services in San Pablo City.

## What

San Pablo City offers comprehensive **maternal and child health services** through barangay health stations and the City Health Office, including:

- **Prenatal check-ups** and pregnancy monitoring
- **Maternal nutrition counseling** and supplementation
- **Tetanus and Hepatitis B vaccination** for pregnant women
- **Family planning** counseling and contraceptive services
- **Birthing facility referrals**
- **Newborn care and newborn screening**
- **Routine child immunization** under DOH's Expanded Program on Immunization (EPI)
- **Catch-up vaccinations** for missed doses
- **Growth monitoring** and nutrition tracking

## Who can avail

Open to:

- **Pregnant women** of all ages residing in San Pablo City
- **Mothers** of newborns (0–6 weeks postpartum)
- **Children 0–5 years old** for routine immunization and growth monitoring
- **School-age children** for catch-up vaccinations
- **Adolescent mothers** with priority counseling support
- **PhilHealth members** for enhanced coverage

## When services are scheduled

| Service | Typical Schedule |
|---|---|
| **Prenatal check-ups** | Tuesdays / Thursdays at most barangay health centers (varies) |
| **Routine immunization** | Wednesdays or scheduled days per barangay |
| **Newborn screening** | Within 24–72 hours after birth (at hospital or RHU) |
| **EPI Mass Immunization Days** | Announced via City Health Office and barangays |
| **Family planning consultation** | Continuous during regular clinic hours |

> Prenatal: Schedule your **first visit before 12 weeks of pregnancy** for best outcomes.

## Where to go

| Service Need | Where |
|---|---|
| **Routine prenatal** | Your barangay health station |
| **High-risk pregnancy** | City Health Office or hospital OB-GYN |
| **Childbirth** | Designated birthing facility or hospital (per referral) |
| **Newborn screening** | Hospital where baby was born or barangay RHU |
| **Routine vaccines (0–5 yrs)** | Barangay health station |
| **Catch-up / school vaccines** | Barangay health station or school health program |

## Why prioritize maternal and child health

- **Reduces maternal mortality** — Early prenatal detection of complications saves lives
- **Reduces infant mortality** — Vaccines prevent measles, polio, hepatitis, etc.
- **Free or subsidized** — Most services are free to San Pablo residents
- **PhilHealth coverage** — Maternity Care Package and newborn package available
- **DOH-mandated programs** — Standardized care across the country
- **Critical for child development** — Nutrition + immunization = healthy growth

## Maternal care services to ask about

- **Prenatal check-ups** — Monitoring weight, blood pressure, fetal growth
- **Tetanus toxoid (TT) vaccination** for pregnant women
- **Iron, folic acid, and calcium supplementation**
- **Blood pressure and weight monitoring**
- **Family planning counseling** (natural and modern methods)
- **Referral to birthing facilities or hospitals** for delivery
- **Postnatal care** and newborn assessment

## Child immunization services (EPI schedule)

| Age | Vaccines |
|---|---|
| **At birth** | BCG, Hepatitis B birth dose |
| **6 weeks** | Pentavalent 1, OPV 1, PCV 1, Rotavirus 1 |
| **10 weeks** | Pentavalent 2, OPV 2, PCV 2, Rotavirus 2 |
| **14 weeks** | Pentavalent 3, OPV 3, IPV, PCV 3 |
| **9 months** | MMR / MR 1 |
| **12 months** | MMR / MR 2 |
| **12–23 months** | Japanese Encephalitis (in some areas) |

> Schedule may vary slightly. Always follow your barangay health worker's guidance.

## How to access services

### Step 1 — Register early at your barangay health center
- Pregnant women: Register **before 12 weeks** of pregnancy
- New parents: Register your newborn **within the first month**

### Step 2 — Bring required documents
- **Mother's valid ID**
- **Prenatal record** or "Mother's Book"
- **Child's birth certificate** (PSA copy or local civil registry)
- **Vaccination card** (issued at birth or first visit)
- **PhilHealth details** (MDR), if available
- **Existing medical records** for high-risk pregnancies

### Step 3 — Attend all scheduled visits
- Don't skip prenatal appointments
- Maintain the immunization schedule strictly
- Catch up on missed doses as soon as possible

### Step 4 — Discuss your delivery plan
Ask your health center about:
- **Birth Plan** preparation
- **Where to deliver** (RHU, birthing center, hospital)
- **What to bring on delivery day**
- **Emergency referral pathways**

### Step 5 — Post-birth — newborn care
After birth:
- Complete **newborn screening** within 24–72 hours
- Get **first immunizations** (BCG, Hep B birth dose)
- Schedule routine well-baby check-ups
- Continue **breastfeeding support** programs

## Emergency reminder

For maternal emergencies — severe bleeding, seizures, severe headache, blurred vision, severe abdominal pain, difficulty breathing — **call 911** or go to the nearest hospital ER immediately.
