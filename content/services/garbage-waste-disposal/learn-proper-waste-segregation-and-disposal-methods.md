# Learn Proper Waste Segregation and Disposal Methods — San Pablo City

A complete guide to proper waste segregation, disposal, and recycling in San Pablo City under RA 9003 (Ecological Solid Waste Management Act).

## What

**Waste segregation** is the practice of sorting waste by type for proper handling — composting, recycling, disposal, or special treatment. San Pablo City follows national waste segregation standards under **RA 9003** (Ecological Solid Waste Management Act of 2000), with local enforcement by barangays and the City Environment & Natural Resources Office (CENRO).

Proper segregation:

- Reduces total waste sent to landfills
- Recovers resources through recycling
- Composts organic matter
- Protects waterways, especially the **Seven Lakes**
- Reduces flooding and pest problems

## Who must segregate

**All San Pablo City residents and businesses** are required to segregate waste under RA 9003. This includes:

- **Households**
- **Businesses** (offices, restaurants, shops)
- **Schools**
- **Hospitals and clinics** (with special handling for medical waste)
- **Markets and food establishments**
- **Construction sites**
- **Special events**
- **Industrial operations**

## When to segregate

| When | What to Do |
|---|---|
| **Daily** | Sort waste at point of generation (kitchen, office, etc.) |
| **Before collection day** | Final sorting and bagging |
| **At public events** | Provide segregated bins |
| **At work / school** | Use designated bins |
| **Year-round** | Segregation is continuous |

## Where to dispose of segregated waste

| Type of Waste | Where to Dispose |
|---|---|
| **Biodegradable** | Compost at home OR barangay collection |
| **Recyclable** | Junk shop OR Materials Recovery Facility (MRF) OR barangay collection |
| **Residual** | Sealed bag for regular barangay collection |
| **Special waste** | Special collection drives or DENR-accredited haulers (see hazardous waste page) |
| **Construction debris** | Coordinate with barangay for special pickup |

## Why segregate

- **Required by law** — RA 9003 (Ecological Solid Waste Management Act)
- **Protects health** — reduces pests and disease
- **Saves space** — recycled materials don't fill up landfills
- **Generates income** — recyclables can be sold to junk shops
- **Reduces pollution** — proper disposal protects waterways
- **Lake protection** — Seven Lakes are vulnerable to runoff
- **Climate impact** — composting reduces methane emissions
- **Civic duty** — every household contributes to a cleaner city

## Basic segregation chart

| Type | Examples | What to Do |
|---|---|---|
| **Biodegradable** | Food scraps, leaves, garden waste, peelings | Compost when possible OR follow barangay collection rules |
| **Recyclable** | Clean bottles, cans, paper, cardboard, plastics | Rinse, dry, set aside for junk shop, MRF, or collection |
| **Residual** | Contaminated packaging, sachets, used tissues | Bag securely for regular collection |
| **Special waste** | Batteries, bulbs, paint, chemicals, electronics, medical waste | DON'T mix with household garbage; ask barangay for proper disposal |

## How to segregate properly

### Step 1 — Set up segregation bins at home
Use **at least 4 separate containers**:
- 🟢 **Biodegradable** (green)
- 🔵 **Recyclable** (blue)
- ⚫ **Residual** (black/gray)
- 🟡 **Special waste** (yellow)

Label clearly so all household members know which bin to use.

### Step 2 — Segregate at point of generation
The moment you create waste, sort it:
- **Kitchen**: peelings → biodegradable; cans → recyclable
- **Bathroom**: tissues → residual; broken glass → labeled
- **Living areas**: paper → recyclable; sachets → residual

### Step 3 — Process biodegradable waste
- **Compost at home** if you have garden space
  - Use a covered bin or pit
  - Add browns (leaves) and greens (food scraps)
  - Turn weekly
  - Use compost for plants in 2–3 months
- **Or set out** for barangay collection on the right day

### Step 4 — Prepare recyclables
Before recycling:
- **Rinse** clean
- **Dry** thoroughly
- **Flatten** boxes and bottles
- **Remove labels** if required by junk shop
- **Sort by type** (paper, plastic, glass, metal)

### Step 5 — Bag residual waste securely
- Use **strong garbage bags**
- **Tie tightly** to prevent leaks
- Set out only on collection day

### Step 6 — Set aside special waste
- **Don't mix** with regular waste
- **Store in safe location** away from children/pets
- **Wait for special collection** drives or take to authorized drop-off

## Household tips

- **Keep separate bins or sacks** at home
- **Drain wet waste** before disposal
- **Flatten boxes and bottles** to save space
- **Label sharp items and broken glass**
- **Never burn garbage** — illegal and harmful
- **Keep waste away** from canals, lakes, waterways
- **Use reusables** to reduce waste at source
- **Compost food scraps** if you have space

## For businesses

Food businesses, markets, clinics, repair shops, and other establishments may have **additional disposal requirements**:

- **Restaurants** — separate food waste; consider composting
- **Clinics / hospitals** — coordinate with DOH-licensed medical waste handlers
- **Auto repair / garages** — use DENR-accredited oil and chemical disposers
- **Salons / barbershops** — segregate hair, nail trimmings, chemicals
- **Construction** — coordinate special pickup or DENR-accredited debris hauler

Ask the **Business Permits and Licensing Office (BPLO)**, your barangay, or relevant agency before disposing of regulated waste.

## Local environmental context

San Pablo is officially known as the **City of Seven Lakes**. **Keep waste away from**:

- Sampaloc Lake
- Palakpakin Lake
- Bunot Lake
- Pandin Lake
- Yambo Lake
- Muhikap (Mohicap) Lake
- Calibato Lake
- Canals, creeks, drainage lines

Even small bits of trash can flow into the lakes during heavy rain, harming aquatic life and tourism.
