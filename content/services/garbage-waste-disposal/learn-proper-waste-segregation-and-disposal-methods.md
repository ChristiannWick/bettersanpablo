# Guidelines for Proper Waste Segregation and Disposal - San Pablo City

Proper waste segregation helps keep San Pablo City clean, reduces flooding and pests, and supports Republic Act 9003, the Ecological Solid Waste Management Act.

## Basic Segregation

| Type | Examples | What to Do |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Biodegradable | food scraps, leaves, garden waste | compost when possible or follow barangay collection rules |
| Recyclable | clean bottles, cans, paper, cardboard, plastics | rinse, dry, and set aside for junk shop, MRF, or barangay collection |
| Residual | contaminated packaging, sachets, used tissues | bag securely for regular collection |
| Special waste | batteries, bulbs, paint, chemicals, electronics, medical waste | do not mix with household garbage; ask barangay or city office for proper disposal |

## Household Tips

- Keep separate bins or sacks at home.
- Drain wet waste before disposal.
- Flatten boxes and bottles to save space.
- Label sharp items and broken glass.
- Never burn garbage.
- Keep waste away from canals, lakes, and waterways.

## For Businesses

Food businesses, markets, clinics, repair shops, and other establishments may have additional disposal requirements. Ask the Business Permits and Licensing Office, barangay, or relevant regulating agency before disposing of regulated waste.

## Local Environmental Context

San Pablo is officially known as the City of Seven Lakes. Keep waste away from lake areas, canals, creeks, and drainage lines. The city's history page names the seven lakes as Sampaloc, Palakpakin, Mohicap, Yambo, Pandin, Calibato, and Bunot.
