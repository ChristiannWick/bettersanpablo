# Report Illegal Dumping or Waste Management Violations — San Pablo City

A guide to reporting illegal dumping, open burning, and waste management violations in San Pablo City.

## What

**Illegal dumping** and waste management violations harm public health, drainage, and San Pablo City's lakes and environment. Reportable violations include:

- **Garbage dumping** on roadsides, vacant lots, canals, creeks, lake areas
- **Open burning** of waste
- **Repeated non-segregation** or blocked collection points
- **Construction debris** dumped without approval
- **Chemical, medical, or hazardous waste** improper disposal
- **Businesses disposing waste** outside authorized channels
- **Dumping in lake areas** or waterways
- **Illegal compost / waste sites**

## Who can report

**Any San Pablo City resident or visitor** can report violations:

- **Residents** witnessing dumping in their barangay
- **Tourists** noticing waste in lake areas
- **Workers and students** observing violations
- **Property owners** whose property is being dumped on
- **Concerned citizens** wanting to protect the environment
- **Anonymous reporters** are accepted by most channels

## When to report

| Situation | Urgency |
|---|---|
| **Dumping happening right now** | Report immediately to barangay |
| **Burning waste** | Call CDRRMO + BFP immediately |
| **Toxic / hazardous spillage** | Call 911 + CDRRMO 0998 540 7171 |
| **Repeated dumping** | Document over time, then report |
| **After-the-fact dumping** | Report with photos and time |
| **Non-segregation** | Educate first, then escalate if persistent |
| **Lake area dumping** | Report immediately (high priority) |

## Why report

- **Protects public health** — Garbage = pests, diseases
- **Saves the lakes** — Seven Lakes are vulnerable to runoff
- **Prevents flooding** — Blocked drainage = flooding
- **Reduces pollution** — Especially toxic chemicals
- **Enforces RA 9003** — Ecological Solid Waste Management Act
- **Sets community standards** — Encourages proper disposal
- **Avoids legal liability** — Property owners can be charged for dumping
- **Civic duty** — Every report contributes to a cleaner city

## How to report

### Step 1 — Document the violation safely
- **Take photos or video** ONLY if it is safe
- **Don't confront** the person directly — let authorities handle it
- **Note from a safe distance** — observe, don't engage
- **Stay back** if hazardous materials are involved

### Step 2 — Record key details
- **Date and time** of the incident
- **Exact location** with landmarks
- **Description of waste** (regular, construction, hazardous)
- **Volume / quantity** estimate
- **Vehicle** plate numbers (if applicable)
- **Business / individual identifying details** if visible
- **Photos / video** evidence

### Step 3 — Report to your barangay first
For most local violations, **start with your barangay hall**:
- Speak with the **Punong Barangay** or **Barangay Tanod**
- Provide all documented details
- Ask for **incident report number** if available
- Request **immediate action** for ongoing violations

### Step 4 — Escalate if needed
If barangay action is insufficient:

- **City Information Office:** (049) 561-1483
- **CENRO** for environmental violations
- **Hotline 8888** for citizen complaints
- **DENR Region IV-A** for major environmental cases

### Step 5 — For emergencies, call right away
- **Burning / fire:** BFP (049) 562-4321 + 911
- **Toxic / chemical:** CDRRMO 0998 540 7171 + 911
- **Major road blockage:** PNP (049) 562-8765 + 911

### Step 6 — Follow up on your report
- **Ask for status** within 7 days
- **Provide additional info** if requested
- **Document non-action** for further escalation
- **Submit photos** of resolution if violation is cleared

## Information to include in your report

When making a report, provide:

- Your **name** and **contact number** (or anonymous if preferred)
- **Date, time, and exact location** of violation
- **Description** of waste / activity
- **Estimated volume**
- **Vehicle / business / individual** identifying info (if visible)
- **Photos / video** evidence
- **Health or safety risks** if applicable
- **Whether ongoing** or already happened

## Emergency situations

Call **911** or **CDRRMO 0998 540 7171** if the dumped material is:

- 🔥 **Burning** or smoking
- 💧 **Leaking** liquids or chemicals
- ☣️ **Toxic** or smells dangerous
- 🚧 **Blocking a major road** or path
- 🌊 **Threatening a waterway** or lake
- 🏥 **Medical waste** (sharps, body fluids)
- 💥 **Containers under pressure** or could explode

## Lake area reports — extra priority

Reports near **Sampaloc, Palakpakin, Mohicap, Yambo, Pandin, Calibato, or Bunot Lake** should include:

- **Nearest lake landmark**
- **Photos** if safe to take
- **Whether waste is entering water** or drainage
- **Type of waste** (especially chemicals or oils)
- **Volume** entering the lake

Lake violations are **high priority** because:
- Lakes are protected ecosystems
- Recovery is slow and expensive
- Tourism and fishing livelihoods depend on lake health
- Wildlife and fish are directly affected
