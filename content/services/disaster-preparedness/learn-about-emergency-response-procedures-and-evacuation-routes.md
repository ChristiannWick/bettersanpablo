# Learn About Emergency Response Procedures and Evacuation Routes — San Pablo City

A guide to understanding emergency response procedures, evacuation routes, and what to do during disasters in San Pablo City.

## What

San Pablo City CDRRMO and barangay disaster teams maintain **emergency response procedures and evacuation routes** for various disaster scenarios. This includes:

- **Designated evacuation centers** in each of the 80 barangays
- **Primary and alternate routes** from each purok / sitio
- **Pick-up points** for rescue and transport
- **Warning signals** specific to each barangay
- **Coordination protocols** between barangays and city
- **Special procedures** for seniors, PWDs, pregnant residents, children, pets

## Who needs to know

Every San Pablo City resident, especially:

- **Heads of households** — primary decision-makers during emergencies
- **Caregivers** of seniors, infants, PWDs
- **Tourists and visitors** — Seven Lakes, hospitality
- **Workers commuting** to and from work
- **Students** going to school
- **Pet owners and livestock keepers**
- **Drivers and transport operators**

## When to confirm your route

| When | Why |
|---|---|
| **Before typhoon season** (May–June) | Routes may have changed due to construction |
| **After major road or drainage works** | Old routes may be blocked or unsafe |
| **When moving to a new address** | New barangay assignments |
| **Annually as a refresher** | Maintain awareness |
| **After felt earthquakes** | Aftershock readiness |
| **Before lake / mountain trips** | Tourist safety |

## Why it matters

- **Saves lives** — Knowing the right route can mean survival
- **Reduces panic** — Practice + preparation = calm response
- **Helps neighbors** — Vulnerable people depend on coordination
- **Prevents accidents** — Safer routes avoid hazards
- **Improves recovery** — Faster post-disaster registration and aid
- **Mandated by law** — RA 10121 (DRRM Act of 2010)

## What to ask your barangay

When you visit your Barangay Hall, ask:

- **Where is the nearest evacuation center?**
- **What is the primary route from my home?**
- **What is the alternate route if the primary is blocked?**
- **Where is the pick-up point** for rescue or transport?
- **What warning signals does the barangay use?** (siren, bell, megaphone)
- **Who is the contact person** for seniors, PWDs, pregnant residents, children?
- **Where do I bring pets or livestock** if evacuation is needed?
- **Are there special procedures** for the lake-area or hillside areas?

## How to prepare

### Step 1 — Map out your routes
- **Primary route** — most direct path from home to evacuation center
- **Secondary route** — alternate if primary is blocked
- **Walking time** — how long it takes
- **Vehicle access** — for those with cars
- **Public transport** — for those without vehicles

### Step 2 — Identify your evacuation center
- Most barangays use **public schools** or **multipurpose halls**
- Lake-adjacent areas may have **higher ground assembly points**
- Tourist destinations have **dedicated emergency shelters**

### Step 3 — Prepare a household checklist
**Documents:**
- Valid IDs (and photocopies in waterproof bag)
- Insurance / property deeds
- Birth certificates and contracts

**Medical:**
- Maintenance medicines and prescriptions
- First aid kit
- Special needs supplies

**Sustenance:**
- 3 days of drinking water
- Ready-to-eat food
- Baby formula / supplies

**Essentials:**
- Phone, charger, power bank
- Flashlight + batteries
- Clothes (3 days)
- Hygiene kit + face masks
- Whistle and multi-tool

**Emergency:**
- Printed list of contacts
- Cash (small bills)
- Pet supplies if applicable

### Step 4 — Educate household members
- Make sure **every adult and older child** knows:
  - Emergency hotlines
  - Evacuation routes
  - Meeting place if separated
  - Who to call for help

### Step 5 — Practice the routes
- Walk or drive your routes **at least twice a year**
- Time yourselves
- Note any new obstacles
- Update Go-Bag regularly

### Step 6 — Build neighborhood awareness
- Know your **neighbors' contact info**
- Identify **vulnerable persons** who need assistance
- Coordinate with **purok leaders** and **barangay tanods**
- Volunteer for barangay **DRRM activities**

## Critical procedures during disasters

### During an earthquake
1. **Duck, Cover, Hold** — under sturdy furniture
2. Stay **away from windows, glass, falling objects**
3. After shaking stops — **carefully evacuate** to open assembly area
4. Check for **injuries and damage**
5. Listen to **PHIVOLCS aftershock advisories**

### During a flood
1. Move to **higher ground** immediately
2. **Avoid floodwaters** — even 6 inches can knock you down
3. Don't drive through flooded roads
4. Watch for **electrical hazards** (downed wires)
5. Listen to **CDRRMO and PAGASA advisories**

### During a fire
1. **Get out, stay out**
2. Crawl low under smoke
3. Touch doors before opening (heat = fire on other side)
4. **Call BFP:** (049) 562-4321
5. Meet at the assembly point — **never go back inside**

### During a typhoon
1. **Evacuate early** when ordered
2. Stay indoors during peak winds
3. **Charge devices** before power outages
4. Follow **CDRRMO and barangay** advisories
5. Don't venture out until **all-clear signal**

## Important notes

- **Confirm routes annually** — they change with construction, road repairs
- **Lake-area residents** have specific evacuation procedures
- **Vehicle access** may be limited during heavy flooding
- **Pets and livestock** — have a separate plan; some shelters accept pets
- **Don't ignore evacuation orders** — they save lives
- **Stay calm** and follow official instructions
