# Access Disaster Preparedness Information and Early Warning Systems — San Pablo City

A guide to monitoring official disaster advisories and early warning systems in San Pablo City through CDRRMO, barangay channels, and partner agencies.

## What

San Pablo City's **CDRRMO (City Disaster Risk Reduction and Management Office)** issues official advisories about:

- **Typhoons, rainfall, and flooding** — coordinated with PAGASA
- **Earthquakes** — relayed from PHIVOLCS
- **Volcanic activity** — Mt. Banahaw and surrounding monitoring
- **Lake area restrictions** — Seven Lakes safety advisories
- **Road closures and traffic** — during emergencies
- **Class and work suspensions** — released by City Hall
- **Evacuation orders** — pre-emptive and emergency
- **Relief distribution schedules**

## Who should monitor

All San Pablo City residents, especially:

- **Households in flood-prone barangays** (lake-adjacent areas)
- **Residents in landslide-prone areas** (hillsides, Mt. Cristobal foothills)
- **Families with seniors, PWDs, infants, pregnant women**
- **Public utility operators** (water, transport, market)
- **Schools, hospitals, and businesses**
- **Tourists visiting Seven Lakes** during typhoon season
- **Barangay officials and tanods**

## When to monitor advisories

| Period | Why |
|---|---|
| **Daily during typhoon season** (June–November) | Increased rainfall, flooding |
| **Before scheduled travel** to lake areas or rural barangays | Road / safety advisories |
| **After heavy rainfall** | Landslide / flash flood risk |
| **After felt earthquakes** | PHIVOLCS aftershock advisories |
| **During declared emergencies** | Real-time updates |
| **Year-round for daily safety bulletins** | General awareness |

## Why use official sources

- **Verified accuracy** — Official sources are reliable
- **Timely updates** — Real-time during disasters
- **Avoids panic** — Misinformation spreads quickly on social media
- **Specific to San Pablo** — Local advisories are more useful than national
- **Coordinated response** — Aligns with city emergency operations
- **Legal weight** — Class suspensions, evacuation orders are enforceable

## What to verify during an alert

When you receive an advisory, **always confirm**:

- **Affected barangays or roads**
- **Evacuation area** and transport instructions
- **Class, market, public office suspensions**
- **Electricity or water service** disruption
- **Relief distribution** location and schedule
- **Safe routes** for travel
- **Special instructions** for vulnerable groups

## How to stay informed

### Step 1 — Save emergency hotlines
Add these to your phone contacts immediately:
- **CDRRMO Emergency:** 0998 540 7171
- **CDRRM Office:** (049) 800-0405
- **National Emergency:** 911
- **PNP San Pablo:** (049) 562-8765
- **BFP San Pablo:** (049) 562-4321

### Step 2 — Follow official channels
- **Like / follow** the City Government of San Pablo Facebook page
- **Bookmark** sanpablocity.gov.ph
- **Save** PAGASA and PHIVOLCS websites
- **Subscribe** to your barangay's official channels

### Step 3 — Coordinate with your barangay
- Know your **Punong Barangay's contact**
- Identify the **barangay tanod / volunteer**
- Locate your nearest **evacuation center**
- Understand the **barangay warning signals**

### Step 4 — Verify before sharing
- **Check the date** of any advisory you receive
- **Cross-reference** with official channels
- **Don't forward** unverified screenshots
- **Report fake news** to City Information Office

### Step 5 — Prepare your household
- Maintain a **family Go-Bag** (3 days of essentials)
- Discuss your **evacuation plan** with family members
- Know who to contact for **seniors, PWDs, infants**
- Charge devices and have **power banks** ready

### Step 6 — Act when advised
- Follow **evacuation orders** promptly
- Don't return to evacuated areas until cleared
- Cooperate with barangay tanods and rescuers
- Help neighbors in need

## Important notes

- **Do not rely on forwarded screenshots alone** — verify with official channels
- **CDRRMO is the city authority** for disaster information
- **Barangay-level** advisories are most relevant to your specific area
- **Mobile signal** may be disrupted during disasters — keep radio handy
- Misinformation can be a **criminal offense** — share responsibly
