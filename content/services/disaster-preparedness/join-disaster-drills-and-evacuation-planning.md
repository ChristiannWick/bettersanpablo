# Join Disaster Drills and Evacuation Planning — San Pablo City

A complete guide to participating in barangay-level disaster drills and family evacuation planning in San Pablo City.

## What

San Pablo City CDRRMO and barangays organize **regular disaster drills and evacuation planning activities** to prepare residents for:

- **Floods** — particularly in lake-adjacent and low-lying barangays
- **Typhoons** — high winds and heavy rainfall
- **Earthquakes** — Duck-Cover-Hold drills and post-quake evacuation
- **Fires** — structural and forest fire response
- **Volcanic activity** — Mt. Banahaw monitoring
- **Landslides** — hillside and Mt. Cristobal foothill areas

San Pablo's **80 barangays** face varied risks based on location.

## Who should participate

Drills are open to:

- **All household members** — adults, children, seniors
- **Barangay tanods and volunteers**
- **Schools and students** — Brigada Eskwela
- **Workplaces and businesses** — workplace safety drills
- **Resort and tourism operators** — Seven Lakes safety
- **Hospital staff** — emergency medical response
- **Persons with special needs** — coordinated assistance

## When drills are held

| Type of Drill | Frequency |
|---|---|
| **National Simultaneous Earthquake Drill (NSED)** | Quarterly (March, June, September, November) |
| **Barangay flood drills** | Pre-typhoon season (May–June) |
| **Fire drills** | Twice yearly per workplace / school |
| **Tsunami / Lake surge drills** | Annual (lake-adjacent barangays) |
| **Family preparedness orientations** | Per barangay schedule |

## Where drills are conducted

| Drill Type | Location |
|---|---|
| **Earthquake drills** | Schools, offices, public spaces (citywide) |
| **Flood drills** | Lake-adjacent barangays, low-lying areas |
| **Fire drills** | Workplaces, schools, public buildings |
| **Community drills** | Barangay halls, plazas, evacuation centers |
| **Family drills** | At home, with all household members |

## Why participate

- **Save lives** — Practice = faster, safer response
- **Know your route** before disaster strikes
- **Identify hazards** in your specific neighborhood
- **Help vulnerable** — seniors, children, PWDs need extra coordination
- **Mandated by law** — RA 10121 requires LGUs to conduct DRRM activities
- **Builds community** — Strengthens neighborhood networks
- **Test your Go-Bag** — Practice tells you if you're really ready

## Types of drills explained

### 🌊 Flood evacuation drill
- Practice evacuating from low-lying or lake-adjacent areas
- Test routes to higher ground
- Coordinate with barangay rescue teams
- Identify pickup points for vulnerable residents

### 🌍 Earthquake drill (NSED)
- **"Duck, Cover, Hold"** practice
- Post-quake evacuation to **open assembly areas**
- Check for injuries and structural damage
- Coordinate with neighbors and barangay

### 🔥 Fire drill
- Response to structural fires (homes, offices)
- Use of fire extinguishers
- Evacuation routes and assembly points
- Coordination with BFP

### 📚 Community DRRM orientation
- General preparedness education
- Hazard mapping for your barangay
- Emergency contacts and signals
- Family Go-Bag preparation

## How to participate

### Step 1 — Contact your Barangay Hall
Ask about:
- Next scheduled **drill or preparedness activity**
- Your assigned **evacuation site** (per purok / sitio)
- **Barangay warning signals** (siren, bell, megaphone codes)
- **Contact persons** for vulnerable groups

### Step 2 — Identify your evacuation center
San Pablo has designated **evacuation centers** across all 80 barangays. Common types:
- Public school gymnasiums
- Barangay multipurpose halls
- Churches (non-denominational use)
- Government buildings (raised structures)

### Step 3 — Plan your evacuation route
- **Primary route** from your home
- **Secondary / alternate route** in case primary is blocked
- **Pick-up points** for rescue or transport
- **Meeting place** for family members separated during disaster

### Step 4 — Prepare your Family Go-Bag
Keep a ready Go-Bag containing:

| Category | Items |
|---|---|
| **Documents** | Valid IDs, insurance, deed (waterproof bag) |
| **Water and food** | 3 days of water + non-perishable food |
| **Health** | Essential medicines, first aid kit, prescription glasses |
| **Power** | Phone charger, power bank, flashlight, batteries |
| **Comfort** | Extra clothing, blanket, hygiene kit |
| **Emergency** | Cash (small bills), whistle, multi-tool |
| **Contacts** | Printed list of emergency numbers |
| **Special** | Baby supplies, pet food, mobility aids if needed |

### Step 5 — Conduct family drills at home
- **Practice the route** at least twice yearly
- **Time yourselves** — how fast can everyone get out?
- **Assign roles** — who carries what, who checks on whom
- **Quiz children** on emergency hotlines
- **Update Go-Bag** every 6 months (rotate food, water)

### Step 6 — Stay engaged with your barangay
- **Volunteer** for community drills if able
- **Attend** all scheduled barangay assemblies
- **Help vulnerable neighbors** plan their evacuation
- **Report** unsafe areas or blocked routes

## Family drill checklist

- [ ] Everyone knows the **emergency hotlines** (911, CDRRMO, barangay)
- [ ] Family **meeting place** is identified
- [ ] **Go-Bag** is prepared and stored accessibly
- [ ] All members know the **evacuation routes**
- [ ] Special arrangements for **seniors, children, PWDs, pets**
- [ ] **Important documents** are in waterproof storage
- [ ] **Insurance and contracts** are accessible
- [ ] **Phone charged** + power bank ready
- [ ] **Cash** in small bills is set aside

## Important notes

- **Drills are FREE** — no payment to participate
- **Children should join** age-appropriate drills with parents
- **Special needs** — coordinate with barangay focal person
- **Pets and livestock** — have a plan; some shelters allow pets
- **Stay calm during real events** — drills build muscle memory
- **Disabilities and disabilities access** — request reasonable accommodations
