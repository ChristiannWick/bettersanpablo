# Report Illegal Logging, Quarrying, or Fishing — San Pablo City

A guide to reporting environmental violations that damage San Pablo City's lake areas, waterways, slopes, roads, farms, and residential communities.

## What

Report **environmental violations** that damage natural resources, including:

- **Tree cutting** without visible authority (illegal logging)
- **Quarrying, hauling, or excavation** in unsafe or unauthorized areas
- **Fish kills, illegal fishing methods** (dynamite, cyanide, electric fishing)
- **Waste entering a lake**
- **Dumping, burning, or chemical discharge** near the Seven Lakes
- **Blocked drainage, erosion, or landslide risk** after works
- **Sand and gravel extraction** in protected areas
- **Encroachment** on protected lake buffer zones
- **Wildlife trafficking** or hunting

## Who can report

**Any San Pablo City resident or visitor** can report:

- **Residents** witnessing violations in their barangay
- **Tourists** noticing damage to lake areas
- **Workers and farmers** observing unsafe activities
- **Hunters and fishermen** reporting illegal practices
- **Concerned citizens** wanting to protect the environment
- **Anonymous reporters** are accepted

| **Quarrying** | Within 1–3 days |

## Why report

- **Protect Seven Lakes ecosystem** — Lakes are vulnerable to pollution
- **Save wildlife** — Illegal hunting / fishing kills species
- **Prevent landslides** — Quarrying causes slope failure
- **Watershed protection** — Trees protect water sources
- **Public safety** — Unauthorized excavation can cause accidents
- **Climate stability** — Forests sequester carbon
- **Future generations** — Sustainable practices preserve resources
- **Legal compliance** — RA 9275 (Clean Water Act), RA 8550 (Fisheries Code), RA 7586 (NIPAS)

## What to report

### 🌳 Illegal logging / tree cutting
- Cutting trees without DENR authority
- Cutting protected tree species
- Cutting in protected areas
- Selling or transporting illegal lumber

### 🪨 Quarrying / excavation
- Quarrying without permits
- Sand and gravel extraction in protected areas
- Hauling without proper papers
- Excavation causing slope or drainage issues

### 🐟 Illegal fishing
- **Dynamite fishing** (RA 8550 violation)
- **Cyanide fishing**
- **Electric fishing**
- **Fishing without permit** in commercial areas
- **Catching protected species**
- **Fishing during closed seasons**

### 🏭 Pollution
- Chemical discharge into lakes
- Industrial waste in waterways
- Oil spills
- Sewage entering lakes
- Toxic burning

### 🦅 Wildlife violations
- Hunting protected species
- Illegal pet trade (animals from the wild)
- Killing migratory birds
- Trafficking endangered species

## How to report

### Step 1 — Document safely
- **Take photos / video** ONLY if it is safe
- **Don't confront** violators
- **Stay at safe distance**
- **Note details** in writing

### Step 2 — Record key information
- **Date and time** of the incident
- **Exact location** (barangay, street, landmark)
- **Description** of activity
- **Vehicle plate numbers** if visible
- **Business names** or identifiers
- **Number of people / volume of activity**
- **Photos / video** evidence

### Step 3 — Report immediately
For ongoing violations:
1. **Call your Barangay Hall** for immediate local action
2. **Escalate** to City Information Office if the issue is recurring or hazardous
3. **For emergencies** — CDRRMO 0998 540 7171 or 911

For after-the-fact violations:
- **Document over time** for stronger case
- **Submit detailed report** to CENRO or DENR

### Step 4 — Provide complete information
When making a report:
- **Your name** and contact (or anonymous)
- **All recorded details**
- **Photos / video evidence**
- **Witnesses** if any
- **Health / safety risks** observed

### Step 5 — Follow up
- **Ask for reference number**
- **Note who received your report**
- **Check status** within 7 days
- **Escalate** if no action taken

### Step 6 — For ongoing concerns
- **Coordinate with neighbors** — group reports get attention
- **File at multiple offices** (CENRO, DENR, barangay)
- **Document over time**
- **Use Hotline 8888** for unresolved cases

## Critical safety reminders

- ⚠️ **Never confront** violators directly
- ⚠️ **Take photos safely** — from distance
- ⚠️ **Don't risk your safety** to gather evidence
- ⚠️ **Report and let authorities handle**

## Information to include

- **Description** of activity
- **Vehicle / business / individual identifiers**

## Local environmental context

San Pablo City is officially the **City of Seven Lakes**: **Sampaloc, Pandin, Bunot, Yambo, Palakpakin, Calibato, Muhikap (Mohicap)**.

These freshwater crater lakes are **protected ecological zones** under local and national environmental laws. Reports near these lakes should:

- Include the **nearest landmark**
- Include **photos** if safe
- Note whether **waste is entering water** or drainage
- Specify **type of waste / activity**
- Include **volume** entering the lake

Lake violations are **HIGH PRIORITY** because:
- Lakes are protected ecosystems
- Recovery is slow and expensive
- Tourism and fishing livelihoods depend on lake health
- Wildlife and fish are directly affected
