# Get Veterinary Services for Livestock — San Pablo City

A guide to veterinary services, livestock vaccination, disease reporting, and animal health support in San Pablo City.

## What

Livestock owners can access **veterinary services** through the city or provincial veterinary office for:

- **Livestock vaccination** (anti-rabies, hog cholera, FMD, etc.)
- **Animal disease reporting**
- **Anti-rabies / animal bite referral** for humans
- **Animal movement / transport** requirements
- **Farm biosecurity** advice
- **Animal welfare** concerns
- **Backyard livestock** support
- **Commercial farm** consultations

## Who can avail

Open to:

- **Livestock owners** in San Pablo City
- **Backyard farmers** with chickens, pigs, goats, cattle
- **Commercial poultry/livestock operations**
- **Pet owners** for some services (rabies vaccination)
- **Schools and institutions** with animals
- **Persons bitten** by animals (rabies post-exposure)
- **Fisherfolk** for aquaculture animal health

## When to seek services

| Situation | Urgency |
|---|---|
| **Animal bite (suspected rabies)** | URGENT — within 24 hours |
| **Sick animal** | Same day |
| **Dead animal (suspicious cause)** | Same day for testing |
| **Routine vaccination** | Per scheduled drives |
| **Pre-movement / transport** | Within validity period |
| **Disease outbreak** | Immediately |
| **New animals introduced** | Within first week |

## Why use veterinary services

- **Public health protection** — Rabies prevention is critical
- **Animal welfare** — Healthy animals are productive animals
- **Disease control** — Prevents outbreak in farms and communities
- **Compliance with laws** — Movement of livestock requires papers
- **Food safety** — Vaccinated livestock = safe meat / dairy
- **Economic protection** — Sick animals = livelihood loss
- **Most services are FREE** or low-cost

## Services to ask about

### 💉 Livestock vaccination
- **Anti-rabies** (dogs, cats)
- **Hog cholera** (pigs)
- **Foot-and-mouth disease (FMD)** (cattle, pigs)
- **Newcastle disease (NCD)** (chickens)
- **Avian influenza** (birds)
- **Hemorrhagic septicemia** (cattle, carabao)

### 🦠 Animal disease reporting
- Suspected disease outbreak
- Mass die-off
- Unusual symptoms
- Suspected zoonotic diseases

### 🩺 Anti-rabies / animal bite for humans
- Post-exposure prophylaxis (PEP)
- Rabies risk assessment
- Wound treatment
- Referral to RABTC (Rabies Animal Bite Treatment Center)

### 🚛 Animal movement / transport
- **Veterinary Health Certificate** for transport
- **Permit to ship** for inter-region movement
- **Quarantine clearance** if applicable

### 🛡️ Farm biosecurity
- Disease prevention strategies
- Farm sanitation
- Visitor / equipment biosecurity
- Disposal of dead animals

## How to access services

### Step 1 — Identify the right office
- **Vaccination drives** → Watch for barangay / city announcements
- **Sick animal** → City Veterinary Services or private vet
- **Animal bite** → Hospital ER + Animal Bite Treatment Center
- **Movement permits** → DA-BAI or provincial vet
- **Disease report** → Barangay + City Veterinary

### Step 2 — Prepare information
| Information | Notes |
|---|---|
| **Owner's valid ID** | Identity verification |
| **Barangay and farm location** | Service mapping |
| **Animal type and number** | Specific species / breed |
| **Photos or symptoms** | If disease suspected |
| **Vaccination or treatment record** | If available |

### Step 3 — Watch for vaccination drives
- **Anti-rabies drives** — typically held at barangay halls
- **Mass vaccination campaigns** for hogs and chickens
- **Free of charge** for most drives

### Step 4 — Report sick animals immediately
- Call your **Barangay Hall** first
- Then escalate to **City Agriculture Office** if needed
- For serious outbreaks, **isolate** the affected animals
- **Don't move** sick animals
- **Don't bury dead animals** until inspected (in some cases)

### Step 5 — For animal bites
1. **Wash the wound** thoroughly with soap and running water for 10–15 minutes
2. Go to the **nearest hospital ER** or **Animal Bite Treatment Center**
3. Get **post-exposure prophylaxis (PEP)** if recommended
4. **Capture / observe the animal** (10 days for dogs/cats)
5. **Don't kill the animal immediately** — observation period helps confirm rabies status

### Step 6 — Maintain animal health records
- Keep **vaccination cards** for each animal
- **Update records** with each vaccination / treatment
- Note **deworming dates**, treatments
- Keep **purchase records** for traceability

## What to prepare

- **Owner's valid ID**
- **Barangay and farm location**
- **Animal type and number of animals**
- **Photos or symptoms** if disease is suspected
- **Vaccination or treatment record** if available

## Important notes

- **Anti-rabies vaccination is critical** — RA 9482 (Anti-Rabies Act of 2007)
- **Animal bites are emergencies** — seek treatment within 24 hours
- **Rabies is fatal** if untreated — never delay PEP
- **Most veterinary services are FREE** or subsidized
- **Reporting outbreaks** is mandatory under the law
- **Animal movement permits** are required for transport
- **Dispose of dead animals properly** — ask vet before burying
